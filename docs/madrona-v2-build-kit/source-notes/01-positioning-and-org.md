# 01 — Positioning & organizational design

## Positioning

- **Who:** A small, senior product/digital studio in Bellingham, WA. Founder:
  Charlie Koch. Model = one senior product lead at the center; a trusted network
  of designers, engineers, researchers comes in as engagements require. Every
  engagement is led personally; the team stays small on purpose.
- **One-liner:** "We help companies figure out what to build, then build it."
- **Who it serves:** Small-to-medium businesses ("good businesses around here")
  across the Pacific Northwest and beyond. Deliberately broad on verticals —
  shop, clinic, outfitter, family farm, nonprofit — not a single industry.
- **Local identity (real, not decorative):** Bellingham / Whatcom County is home
  ("from here"). Farmers market, food bank, land trust. "Neighbors first."
- **Voice:** "We" throughout. Direct, confident, personal, editorial, restrained.
  No corporate tone, no hype, no em-dashes.

## Career/credibility threads

Charlie: ~15 years product leadership at REI, Healthline, Microsoft — at the
junction of three domains: **outdoor advocacy · adventure travel · health &
wellness.** The pitch: plenty of leaders have lived in one; living at the
intersection of all three is the differentiator. (This three-pillar framing is
background identity, not a customer-facing menu.)

## Service architecture — Grow / Retain / Operate

Internal vocabulary is **Grow / Retain / Operate**; customer surfaces never say
those words. Each bucket is presented as a **symptom question ("door")**, an
**impact line in the owner's terms**, and **plain-words offerings**.

1. **Grow — "Getting found."**
   - Door: "Selling something great behind a web presence that doesn't do it justice?"
   - Impact: more people find you, and more of them buy.
   - Offerings: new websites, brand & positioning, content & marketing, online stores.
   - **Flagship: selling online** (e-commerce) — a highlighted callout, copy-first,
     framed around Shopify builds/replatforms, integrations, checkout that converts.
2. **Retain — "Coming back."**
   - Door: "People buy from you once, then you never hear from them again?"
   - Impact: customers come back more often.
   - Offerings: loyalty & memberships, repeat/standing ordering, win-back emails & texts, reviews.
3. **Operate — "Running smoother."**
   - Door: "Watching the week disappear into work that software should be doing?"
   - Impact: hours back every week.
   - Offerings: workflow fixes, small single-job tools, AI agents on real workflows.
   - **Flagship: agentic operations** (`/services/agentic-operations`) — agents +
     one command surface. Worked example is **Berry Good Berry Farm**, openly
     framed as the studio's demonstration business (a cast of agents being built).
     Dogfood proof = the studio's own ops tool, **Helm**.

Cross-cutting principles:
- **Success criteria run through everything** — every engagement names its win up
  front, in the owner's terms, measured. Honest expectation, never a guarantee.
- **"Signal" is method, not a service** — "real customers before real money" lives
  on /how-it-works, not the services menu.

## Engagement model (the front door)

1. **Free 30-minute conversation** with a *published 5-beat agenda* (where you're
   at / where you've been / what "better" looks like / biggest opportunities /
   what's on your mind).
2. **Written assessment** — short, honest, names its measures; client keeps it.
3. **Scoped proposal** — if it makes sense; starts small with visible payback.
   (No prices published currently.)

Proposal *shapes* (distinct from the service architecture; shown on /how-it-works):
Strategy sprint (2–6 wk) · Signal sprint (4–12 wk) · Product stewardship (3–6 mo retainer).

## Studio-as-proof

The studio builds and runs its own products ("building in the open"), and those
double as the case studies (see IA doc). Some exist because they should, not for
a business model — framed as what keeps the work honest.
