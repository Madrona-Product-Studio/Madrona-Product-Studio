# Assessment respec: report-first (2026-08-29)

Working doc for the V3-era redo of the `/checkup` tool. Drafted from the
2026-08-29 session: critique of the current Signal Assessment, teardown of
cxo.dev's AI readiness diagnostic, and the realization that the V3 hero's
autogenerated "example read" card is the output the tool should actually
produce. **The report card is the spec; the questions exist to fill its
slots honestly.**

## Identity sentence

> This tool helps an owner find the one place where modern tools would pay
> off first, and hands them a short read they keep either way.

Everything that doesn't serve that sentence gets cut. It is not a service
router, not a readiness score, not a personality quiz.

### What this fixes (diagnosis of the current /checkup)

- Q2/Q3/Q4 are the same "where's the friction" question three times: 18
  abstract multi-select statements. The reading load is why 7 questions
  feel long.
- Answers are self-assessed vibes, so the user does the diagnostic work
  and the tool just files it into a pathway. Facts should go in and
  insight should come out (the cxo.dev move: anchored, factual answers
  that take 5 seconds each and teach as they're read).
- The output is an archetype + pathway, i.e. "which of our services fits
  you." Category out, not a move out. Nothing measured, nothing kept.
- The founding concept (HQ ai-checkup doc: "it demos the consultancy's
  actual product," i.e. a genuinely generated mini written-assessment)
  was lost in the deterministic ship.

### What stays right

- Free, no email gate to see the result. Email only for "want Charlie's
  personal read?" (the earned capture).
- The ~2-minute promise. Honest labels, no scores (canon ban on the
  "73% AI-ready" register stands; do not copy cxo.dev's scoring/benchmark
  identity, only its question mechanics).
- The readiness question (current Q7) — it sizes the engagement.
- Hero-level placement in V3: the tool is the site's second front door.

## 1. The report card (the centerpiece)

Three panes, exactly as sketched in the V3 hero `DiagnosticCard`
(`src/pages/v3/Hero.tsx`). One React component, two homes:

- **Hero:** rendered with a fixture profile, window bar note "example read"
  stays (honest: real product, example data).
- **Result page:** rendered live from the user's answers, sitting above an
  expanded read.

Single source of truth; the hero tracks the real design forever.

### Pane 1 — Current signals

Four rows, one per door, plain-words status **derived 1:1 from an anchored
answer** so every row is defensible ("you told us"). Each row's vocabulary
includes a genuinely positive state; if every run flags all four doors the
card is a brochure, not a read.

| Row (door) | Status vocabulary (good → bad) |
|---|---|
| Web presence (Brand & Website) | Earning trust · Doing OK · Underselling · Barely there |
| Repeat customers (Growth & Retention) | Coming back · Steady · Leaking away · Unknown |
| Hours lost to admin (AI & Operations) | Under 2 a week · 2–5 a week · 5–12 a week · 12+ a week |
| AI leverage (AI & Operations) | Working already · Getting started · Untapped |

New Products has no fixed row (matches the hero mock). The new-product
answer (Q7 below) feeds the first recommendation, and *optionally* a fifth
row appears only when the answer is hot ("stalled" / "need help building").
Decision open.

### Pane 2 — What better looks like

Two or three target lines for the weakest rows only. Labels from the mock
are right: **Clearer offer → "Clear" · Repeat path → "Connected" · Admin
relief → "Useful."** Each target gets a one-line plain-words meaning on the
full result page (e.g. "Clear: a stranger gets why to choose you in
thirty seconds"). The mock's percentage bars are decorative; replace with
a now→better two-state track or drop the bars. Settle visually in build
(visual QA gate).

### Pane 3 — First recommendation

Singular and workflow-level. Never a service category.

- **Headline:** a verb sentence naming their workflow. Mock register is
  the bar: "Fix the highest-friction handoff before adding another tool."
- **Support line:** "Start with the workflow people already repeat." —
  when the user typed their dread task, this line uses their own words.
- **Why-now line:** the capability that makes it newly solvable ("models
  can now read handwritten notes / draft in your own format / answer from
  your real inventory").

v1 engine: deterministic template library keyed by (dread-task chip ×
weakest door × readiness). Phase 2: live LLM generates the read from the
free text (restores the founding concept; demos the real written
assessment). The deterministic path remains as fallback always.

### Below the card (result page only)

- Expanded read per pane, narrow measure, editorial voice.
- One worked example matched to the pattern (Berry Good for ops patterns;
  the boat-inspection story once public: expert judgment + document
  drudgery).
- CTA: "Talk through this read" → book the free 30 minutes. Secondary:
  keep it (copy link), retake.

## 2. The question set (draft, fills the slots)

Eight questions, all anchored single-selects except Q6. Target under 90
seconds. Copy below is draft-quality; no em-dashes in shipped copy.

1. **Context (keep from current Q1).** "What kind of business are we
   looking at?" Same six options.
2. **Web presence row.** "If a stranger only saw your website, how well
   would they get why to choose you?" — They'd get it right away / Most
   of it / Some of it / Honestly, the business is better than the site /
   We barely have a web presence.
3. **Repeat row.** "Out of ten customers who buy once, how many do you
   hear from again?" — Eight or more / Five to seven / Two to four / One,
   maybe / We don't really know. ("Don't know" maps to status *Unknown* —
   itself a systems signal.)
4. **Hours row.** "In a typical week, how many hours go to work a
   computer should be doing? Scheduling, retyping, chasing, copying
   between tools." — Under 2 / 2 to 5 / 5 to 12 / 12 or more / I've
   stopped counting.
5. **AI leverage row.** "Is AI doing any real work in the business
   today?" — Not at all / We've poked at ChatGPT / It helps with a task
   or two / It's part of daily work / Automation runs on its own.
6. **The workflow finder (the centerpiece).** "What's the task you dread?
   The one that follows the same shape every time and eats hours anyway."
   Optional chips for no-typing completion: Writing up reports or quotes ·
   Invoicing and paperwork · Answering the same questions again ·
   Moving info between tools · Scheduling and coordinating · Something
   else — plus an optional free-text line ("say it in your words and the
   read gets sharper"). The questions should teach the way cxo.dev's do:
   reading the chips tells an owner what's newly possible.
7. **New-product itch.** "Is there something new you keep meaning to make
   real?" — No, we're focused on the business we have / An idea keeps
   coming back / We started, but it stalled / Yes, and we want help
   building it.
8. **Readiness (keep from current Q7, reworded to V3 voice).** "What are
   you ready to do about it?" — same five options.

Cut entirely: the three redundant friction multi-selects, the
interstitial lines ("That's a meaningful signal"), the synthesis theater,
the 8 archetypes, the Signal Brain on this surface (park the component;
it's good craft, wrong job).
