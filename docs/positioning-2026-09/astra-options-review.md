1. **A is my choice.** Its ten-second read is, “They can improve our website and reduce the work that slows us down.” That fits both an owner and a nonprofit director, and gives Charlie’s two lead offers equal billing. Its strongest element is the pairing of a concrete promise with visible design craft. Its weakest element is the long question ledger before the next demonstration, especially on mobile. New products also gets little supporting evidence. The Berry Good demonstration label is clear and appropriately honest.

2. **B makes the strongest visual case for hiring the studio.** Its ten-second read is, “They make attractive websites and products; here is their example business.” The Berry Good brand exhibit is its strongest element because it shows the broader branded experience. Its weakest element is the balance: visitors see Berry Good twice before reaching the workflow walkthrough, whose actual interface is hidden in a hero tab. “One business, improved end to end” suggests an existing business with demonstrated improvement. For a demonstration, “One example, built end to end” would be more accurate.

3. **C makes the scope explicit but asks for too much reading.** Its ten-second read is, “They use AI to build brands, websites, and business systems.” Its strongest element is the direct path from a recognizable problem to its answer. Its weakest element is repetition: six questions in the hero, then those same six questions below, with no visual demonstration of craft above them. “Built with AI, done well” is a quality assertion rather than evidence and makes AI sound like the method for every engagement. That sits awkwardly beside the promise that an existing tool or no build might be the right answer.

4. **All three need a shared honesty pass.** The home inspection label correctly identifies an illustration, but “sends the same day” reads as an established result. Describe the intended workflow without promising that turnaround. “Nothing enters the report unconfirmed” should clearly describe a proposed review requirement. The Healthline sentence still risks implying that the AI guidance launched across the entire 90-million-visitor portfolio. Separate Charlie’s launch responsibility from the portfolio’s audience scale. Microsoft’s attribution through Iconmobile is a good correction.

5. **The mobile topographic background is too prominent in all three.** Contours cross the headline, paragraph, credentials, and secondary action. Desktop largely confines that texture to the visual side; mobile needs a similarly quiet area behind the copy. The consistent side gutters look sound in these captures.

6. **Mobile stacking changes the positioning.** A and C put all three brand questions and answers before any workflow question. Two equal desktop columns become a long website-first argument. C compounds this with a five-line headline and a hero approximately 1,200 pixels tall. B’s first mobile slice is almost entirely Berry Good. Each needs an earlier, visible indication of both lead offers.

7. **Some desktop spacing weakens the hierarchy.** In A and C, the inspection section leaves substantial empty space beneath the left paragraph while the right column continues through four steps. In B, the inspection section feels sparse because those steps appear without the artifact. Across the options, generous section padding repeatedly postpones the next useful piece of information. The strategy row also explains the paid sprint twice in adjacent columns.

8. **Keep revisiting the questions.** “Does your website do your work justice?” and the duplicate-entry question are strong. Split finding you from taking action: “Can people find you online?” and “Is it easy to book, donate, or sign up?” are distinct problems. Consider “Are you losing touch with customers, donors, or members?” for the retention question. “Post-sale follow-up” and “customer email” narrow otherwise inclusive language.

9. **Take A forward, borrowing selectively.** From B, borrow the Berry Good brand exhibit to substantiate branded experiences beyond websites, preferably replacing repeated material. From C, borrow direct links from individual questions to their answers or examples. Keep that useful navigation without repeating the whole question set in the hero.
