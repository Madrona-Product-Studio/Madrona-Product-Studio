# Berry Good Berry Farm brand guide

First edition, Summer 2026.

This is the portable, chat-friendly version of the live Berry Good brand book. Upload this file and the SVG logo files to ChatGPT when developing new materials. The live source remains `/kit/style-guide/` in the Berry Good site.

## Brand idea

Berry Good is a fictional u-pick berry farm in the Nooksack Valley. The creative direction is "roadside stand editorial": farmstand warmth and vernacular, executed with editorial precision.

The brand should feel like a farm, never a technology company. Its world includes flats, rows, flags, the stand, the honor box, the board, peak weeks, and last calls.

## Logo

The mark is a raspberry built from eight drupelet dots and two leaves. It should feel simple enough for a road sign and distinctive enough for a favicon.

Files:

- `berry-good-mark.svg`: preferred full-color mark
- `berry-good-mark-one-color.svg`: one-color mark for constrained reproduction

Usage:

- Keep clear space equal to one drupelet around every side.
- Use the full-color mark on cream, paper, or blackcurrant.
- Use the one-color mark when production allows only one ink.
- Do not stretch, rotate, outline, add effects, or rearrange the drupelets.
- Do not place the full-color mark on raspberry, where the berry disappears.
- The practical minimum size is 24 pixels on screen or 8 millimeters in print.

The primary wordmark is typeset, not embedded in the SVG. Set "Berry Good" or "Berry Good Berry Farm" in Fraunces, semibold, with optical size 144, softness 70, and wonk enabled. Pair it with the raspberry mark. Do not invent a different display face.

## Typography

### Fraunces

Role: the sign painter. Use for headlines and section titles. Set it jammy, with optical size 144, soft terminals at 70, and wonk on. Do not use it for small body copy.

### Karla

Role: the stand window. Use for body copy, buttons, forms, and conversational text. Keep it between 13 and 16 pixels for most interface copy, with a roomy line height.

### Spline Sans Mono

Role: the board and letterboard. Use for statuses, tickers, timestamps, and micro-labels. Keep it small, tracked out, and usually uppercase.

Fallbacks:

- Fraunces: Georgia, serif
- Karla: Helvetica Neue, sans-serif
- Spline Sans Mono: Menlo, monospace

## Voice

1. Use plain punctuation. Periods, commas, and colons. Never use em dashes.
2. Sound like the person at the stand window. Use short sentences and words a person would say aloud.
3. Prefer farmstand words: flats, rows, flags, the stand, the honor box, and the board.
4. Prefer specifics over adjectives. "Rows 4 through 9" is stronger than "select fields."
5. Write as if the information must be true this morning.

Examples:

- Say: "Raspberries hit peak this morning. Come before Sunday."
- Avoid: "Our premium raspberry selection is now available for a limited time!"
- Say: "Pay on your way out. Card, cash, or the honor box."
- Avoid: "We support multiple convenient payment options."
- Say: "Back next June. They were worth it."
- Avoid: "Strawberry season has officially concluded for this year."

## Photography

Show the evidence of the day: fruit on the cane, stained hands, flats on a table, row flags, handwritten boards, the honor box, and morning light. Favor honest crops and natural color. Images should feel observed, not staged.

Avoid generic lifestyle stock, heavy presets, spotless studio arrangements, fake rustic props, and imagery that could belong to any food brand.

## Working with ChatGPT

Attach this guide and the preferred SVG mark, then start with a prompt like:

> Use the attached Berry Good brand guide and logo as constraints. Help me create [deliverable]. Keep the farmstand voice, use only the documented palette and type system, and do not use em dashes. First propose the content hierarchy and copy. Then produce the final asset or implementation.

When reviewing an output, ask ChatGPT to check it against these questions:

1. Does it sound like the stand window?
2. Are the details specific and plausible this morning?
3. Does it use only the documented colors and type roles?
4. Is the logo given enough room and used without alteration?
5. Does it feel like a farm rather than a software company?
6. Is all punctuation compliant with the no-em-dash rule?
